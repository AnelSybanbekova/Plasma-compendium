Particle Drifts in Electromagnetic Fields

In a magnetized plasma, charged particles do not simply follow magnetic field lines. In addition to their gyromotion around magnetic field lines, particles can experience a gradual motion of their guiding center across the magnetic field. This motion is known as drift.

Particle drifts play an important role in plasma physics because they determine particle transport, influence plasma confinement, and contribute to the generation of electric currents and macroscopic plasma flows.

1. What is a Particle Drift?

When a charged particle moves through a magnetic field, the Lorentz force causes it to gyrate around the magnetic field line. In a uniform magnetic field and in the absence of other forces, the center of this circular motion remains approximately fixed.

However, if an additional force acts on the particle, or if the magnetic field is non-uniform, the center of the gyromotion can slowly move across the magnetic field. This slow motion is called guiding-center drift.

The general idea can be represented as

particle motion = gyromotion + guiding-center motion

2. General Lorentz Force

The motion of a charged particle is governed by the Lorentz force:

F = q(E + v × B)

where:

  • q is the particle charge,
  • E is the electric field,
  • v is the particle velocity,
  • B is the magnetic field.

The magnetic part of the Lorentz force is always perpendicular to the particle velocity. Therefore, it changes the direction of the particle's motion rather than directly changing its kinetic energy.

Drift motion appears when the forces acting on the particle are not balanced in such a way that the guiding center remains stationary.

3. E × B Drift

One of the most important drift motions occurs when both an electric field and a magnetic field are present.

The resulting drift velocity is

vE×B = (E × B) / B2

where B = |B|.

An important property of the E × B drift is that it is independent of particle charge and mass.

Therefore, electrons and ions experience the same E × B drift velocity when they are in the same electric and magnetic fields.

The drift is perpendicular to both the electric and magnetic fields:

vE×B ⟂ E

and

vE×B ⟂ B

This drift is particularly important in magnetized plasmas because an electric field can produce a macroscopic plasma flow across magnetic field lines.

4. Gradient-B Drift

In many plasma systems, the magnetic field is not uniform. When the magnitude of the magnetic field varies in space, a charged particle experiences a gradient-B drift.

The magnetic moment of a gyrating particle can be written as

μ = m v⊥2 / (2B)

where v⊥ is the component of velocity perpendicular to the magnetic field.

The gradient-B drift velocity is

v∇B = (μ / qB2) (B × ∇B)

Equivalently, using the perpendicular velocity,

v∇B = (m v⊥2 / 2qB3) (B × ∇B)

The direction of this drift depends on the sign of the particle charge. Therefore, ions and electrons drift in opposite directions.

Gradient-B drift becomes particularly important in magnetic confinement devices where the magnetic field strength changes significantly across the plasma.

5. Curvature Drift

Magnetic field lines in many confinement configurations are curved. A particle moving parallel to a curved magnetic field line experiences an effective centrifugal force.

This force produces another guiding-center drift called curvature drift.

The curvature drift velocity can be written as

vcurv = (m v∥2 / qB) (b × κ)

where

b = B / B

is the unit vector along the magnetic field and κ is the curvature vector of the magnetic field line.

The curvature drift depends on the particle's parallel velocity. Like the gradient-B drift, it has opposite directions for positive and negative charges.

6. Combined Gradient and Curvature Drifts

In a realistic magnetic confinement system, gradient-B and curvature drifts usually occur simultaneously.

The total magnetic drift can therefore be approximated as

vmag = v∇B + vcurv

Both effects are particularly important in toroidal magnetic confinement devices such as tokamaks.

Because the magnetic field in a tokamak is stronger on the inner side of the torus and weaker on the outer side, particles experience both magnetic field gradients and curved field lines.

7. Force Drift

More generally, any force that is perpendicular to the magnetic field can produce a guiding-center drift.

For a perpendicular force F, the drift velocity is

vF = (F × B) / (qB2)

This expression is useful because several important drift mechanisms can be understood as different forms of force drift.

For example, the gradient-B drift can be interpreted as a drift caused by the effective force associated with the magnetic moment:

F∇B = −μ∇B

8. Why Do Electrons and Ions Drift in Opposite Directions?

Most magnetic drifts contain the factor 1/q. Since electrons have a negative charge while ions usually have a positive charge, their drift directions are opposite.

This difference is extremely important in plasma physics. If electrons and ions experience different drift velocities, charge separation and electric currents can develop.

The E × B drift is an important exception: because its velocity does not contain q, electrons and ions move together in the same E × B drift.

9. Drifts and Plasma Confinement

Particle drifts are one of the fundamental challenges of magnetic plasma confinement.

The idealized picture of a charged particle spiraling indefinitely around a magnetic field line is not sufficient for a real plasma. Magnetic field gradients, field-line curvature, electric fields, collisions, and other effects cause the guiding centers to move.

In a toroidal device, such as a tokamak, these drifts can cause particles to move vertically and radially across magnetic surfaces. If these transport processes are not controlled, particles and energy can escape from the confined plasma.

Understanding drift motion is therefore essential for designing magnetic configurations capable of maintaining stable and well-confined plasmas.

10. Summary

Particle drift describes the slow motion of a charged particle's guiding center across magnetic field lines.

  • E × B drift: caused by an electric field and independent of particle charge and mass.
  • Gradient-B drift: caused by spatial variation of magnetic field strength and directed differently for positive and negative charges.
  • Curvature drift: caused by the curvature of magnetic field lines and depends on the particle's parallel velocity.
  • Force drift: a general mechanism describing drift caused by a force perpendicular to the magnetic field.

Together, these drift motions provide an important foundation for understanding particle transport and magnetic confinement in fusion plasmas.
